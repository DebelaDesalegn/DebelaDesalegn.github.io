## 1. Research motivation

Multilingual models such as XLM-R have Latin-centric vocabularies that split Amharic and Tigrinya words, written in the Ge'ez script, into many small pieces. This wastes sequence length and can hurt performance.

## 2. Problem statement

Adapt a pretrained multilingual model to Ge'ez-script languages by extending its vocabulary, and measure the effect on tokenization and on downstream tasks.

## 3. Related work

Vocabulary extension and continued pretraining have been used to adapt multilingual models to new languages; Glot500 broadens coverage to hundreds of languages, but Ge'ez-script segmentation remains inefficient.

## 4. Research question

Does adding language-specific Ge'ez-script subwords to XLM-R improve tokenization and downstream performance, and how much of any gain comes from continued pretraining?

## 5. Methodology

Separate SentencePiece unigram tokenizers were trained for Amharic and Tigrinya, and 30,000 pieces missing from XLM-R were added, growing its vocabulary from 250,002 to 280,002 entries without changing existing ones. New embeddings start at the mean of the original embeddings. Training has two stages: continued masked language modelling on monolingual text, then task fine-tuning.

## 6. Experimental setup

VEXMLM is compared with XLM-R and Glot500, with downstream results averaged over five seeds. An ablation on Tigrinya NER separates vocabulary expansion, initialisation and continued pretraining.

## 7. Datasets

| Task | Amharic | Tigrinya |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Named-entity recognition | MasakhaNER | Tigrinya NER dataset |
| Question answering | AmQA | TIGQA |
| Sentiment analysis | AfriSenti | — |

## 8. Evaluation metrics

Tokenizer fertility (subword tokens per word) and compression; accuracy and F1 for NER; accuracy for sentiment; exact match and F1 for QA.

## 9. Results

| Measure | XLM-R | VEXMLM |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fertility, Amharic | 2.07 | **1.49** (-28.0%) |
| Fertility, Tigrinya | 3.13 | **1.69** (-45.9%) |
| NER accuracy, Amharic | 0.937 | **0.941** |
| NER accuracy, Tigrinya | 0.943 | **0.952** |
| QA F1, Amharic | **58.46** | 48.85 |
| Sentiment accuracy, Amharic | 0.507 | 0.498 |

The ablation shows that expanding the vocabulary alone lowers Tigrinya NER accuracy on out-of-vocabulary words (to about 87%), while the full model with continued pretraining beats the baseline (95.66% versus 94.57%).

## 10. Limitations

Only Amharic and Tigrinya were tested, the XLM-R downstream baseline used a single seed, the pretraining data's origin is undocumented, and the tokenizer is frequency-driven rather than morphology-aware.

## 11. Future research directions

Extending to other Ge'ez-script languages, morphology-aware tokenization, investigating the QA gap, and comparing with AfroXLMR and AfriBERTa under one protocol.
