## 1. Research motivation

Multimodal models are trained mostly on images and captions from a narrow set of cultures and languages. For many African communities, knowledge is carried through local imagery and spoken language, which these models rarely see.

## 2. Problem statement

Measure whether multimodal models can answer culturally grounded questions and translate culturally specific content when given images, native-language text and speech; and, on the generative side, explain which parts of a prompt drive a generated image.

## 3. Related work

Recent cultural VQA benchmarks assess global cultural knowledge but cover few African languages, and none combine native-language text with speech. Multimodal machine translation has studied visual disambiguation, but not broader cultural nuance.

## 4. Research questions

- How well do multimodal LLMs understand African cultural knowledge in images, text and speech?
- Can images act as cultural context for machine translation?

## 5. Methodology

**Afri-MCQA** pairs culturally relevant images with parallel English and native-language questions and answers in both text and speech, collected by native speakers living where each language is spoken. Control experiments separate linguistic competence from cultural knowledge.

**CaMMT** collects image–caption triples with parallel English and regional-language captions and compares vision–language models in text-only and text+image translation settings.

For generative models, see [DiffuSAGE](/projects/explainable-machine-learning), which attributes generated image content to prompt tokens in Stable Diffusion v1.5.

## 6. Experimental setup

Afri-MCQA evaluates open-weight and proprietary multimodal LLMs on multiple-choice and open-ended VQA, in English and native languages, through text and speech. CaMMT evaluates five vision–language models with automatic metrics and human evaluation.

## 7. Datasets

| Dataset | Scale | Released |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Afri-MCQA | 7.5k Q&A pairs, 15 African languages, 12 countries | [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Atnafu/Afri-MCQA) |
| CaMMT | Over 5,800 image–caption triples | [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/villacu/cammt) |

## 8. Evaluation metrics

VQA accuracy for Afri-MCQA; automatic translation metrics and human preference judgements for CaMMT.

## 9. Results

- On Afri-MCQA, models perform poorly across evaluated cultures, with **near-zero accuracy** on open-ended VQA when queried in a native language or through speech, and large gaps between native languages and English in both text and speech.
- On CaMMT, visual context generally **improves translation quality**, especially for culturally specific items, disambiguation and correct gender marking.

## 10. Limitations

Each language is represented by a limited set of images and annotators, so the benchmarks sample rather than cover each culture.

## 11. Future research directions

The Afri-MCQA findings point to speech-first approaches, culturally grounded pretraining and cross-lingual cultural transfer.
