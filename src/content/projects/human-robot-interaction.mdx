## 1. Research motivation

Robots increasingly work in close contact with people, and research on human–robot interaction (HRI) grows by hundreds of papers each year. A structured view of where the field stands, across sectors, was missing.

## 2. Problem statement

Map the current status, trends, challenges and research gaps of HRI across application sectors, and examine the role of AI in advancing it.

## 3. Related work

Earlier reviews covered HRI from single perspectives, such as a particular application sector, safety, interaction experience, human factors, or task planning and programming.

## 4. Research question

What is the state of the art of HRI across application sectors, how can interactions be classified, and how does AI shape the field?

## 5. Methodology

A literature review of 89 papers found through Google Scholar, Scopus, AAAI and IEEE Xplore, prioritising peer-reviewed journals and conference proceedings from IEEE, ACM, AAAI and Springer.

## 6. Analytical framework

Interactions are classified by robot morphology and team composition, human–robot ratio, human role (supervisor, operator, teammate, mechanic, bystander), robot task role, space and time, autonomy level (from teleoperation to peer-to-peer collaboration), and co-working mode (coexistence, cooperation, collaboration).

## 7. Scope

Nine sectors: military, education, industry, entertainment, service, space exploration, healthcare, urban search and rescue, and agriculture.

## 8. Evaluation criteria

Each sector is assessed on maturity, interaction forms, the role of AI, and open challenges.

## 9. Findings

- Higher robot autonomy enables more sophisticated interaction; lower autonomy relies on human control.
- Collaborative robots increasingly work alongside people in industry, and service and healthcare robots are growing, especially for elderly care.
- Industry, healthcare and service are the most mature sectors; agriculture is at an early stage.
- AI, including machine learning, natural language processing, speech recognition, computer vision and cognitive architectures, underpins most recent advances.

## 10. Limitations

The review focuses mainly on IEEE and other top-rated peer-reviewed work, and some sectors had too little research to assess fully.

## 11. Future research directions

Safety and usability standards, personalisation for diverse users, better verbal and non-verbal communication, ethical safeguards, combining symbolic reasoning with learning, and inclusive design.
