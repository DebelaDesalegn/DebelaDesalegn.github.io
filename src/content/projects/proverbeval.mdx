## 1. Research motivation

As evaluation datasets multiply, it is hard to find benchmarks that measure language understanding in low-resource languages rather than subject knowledge. Proverbs are culturally specific and linguistically rich, which makes them a good test of real understanding.

## 2. Problem statement

Build a culturally grounded benchmark for Ethiopian languages and study which factors in the evaluation process itself change LLM results.

## 3. Related work

Most LLM benchmarks are in English or translated from it, and few disentangle language understanding from knowledge of a subject. Evaluation practice for low-resource languages, including prompt language and answer ordering, has received little systematic study.

## 4. Research question

How sensitive are LLM benchmark results for low-resource languages to the order of answer choices, the language of the prompt and choices, and the task format?

## 5. Methodology

Three tasks built from proverbs: choosing the explanation that matches a proverb's meaning (four options), filling in a missing word, and generating a proverb from its description. The evaluation varies prompt language (English or native), choice language, answer order (random shuffles versus always first or always last), translate-test with NLLB-200, and zero- versus five-shot prompting.

## 6. Experimental setup

Models: Meta-LLaMA-3 (8B, 70B), Gemma-2 (9B, 27B), LLaMAX3-8B-Alpaca, Aya-101 and GPT-4o. Open models were scored by log-likelihood with lm-eval; GPT-4o by generation with an answer extractor.

## 7. Dataset

| Language | Meaning MCQ | Fill in the blank | Generation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Amharic | 483 | 494 | 484 |
| Afaan Oromo | 502 | 493 | 502 |
| Tigrinya | 380 | 503 | 380 |
| Ge'ez | 434 | 429 | 434 |
| English | 437 | 462 | 437 |

Released on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/israel/ProverbEval).

## 8. Evaluation metrics

Accuracy for multiple choice and fill in the blank; chrF for generation, with BLEU and TER in the appendix.

## 9. Results

- **Choice order changes accuracy by up to about 50%.** Meta-LLaMA-3-8B averaged about 30.6% with random shuffles, 72.8% when the answer was always first, and 8.7% when it was always last.
- Native-language prompts generally scored lower than English prompts.
- Bigger models were not always better; better tokenization of these languages correlated with stronger performance.
- Aya-101 led on multiple choice (about 57.3% with English prompts); GPT-4o had the best generation chrF (about 10.8).
- Native-language descriptions produced better proverbs than English ones, and translating proverbs into English did not help significantly.

## 10. Limitations

Open and closed models are not directly comparable because they were scored differently, only label-based scoring was used, language coverage was limited by volunteer availability, and a small set of LLMs was evaluated.

## 11. Future research directions

Wider language and cultural coverage, more open and closed models, and using these findings to design more robust multilingual benchmarks and models.
